ATLAS is the largest detector at the Large Hadron Collider: 46 metres long and 25 metres high, about half the size of Notre-Dame Cathedral in Paris. Like CMS it is a general-purpose detector, and in 2012 the two together discovered the Higgs boson. Its name comes from its eight huge **toroid magnets**, which bend the paths of muons in the outer part of the detector.

Inside, an **inner detector** in a solenoid magnet follows charged particles, a **liquid-argon calorimeter** measures the energy of electrons and photons, and a **tile calorimeter** made of steel and plastic scintillator measures the energy of everything else. The outer **muon chambers** measure the muons bent by the toroids.

## What I did here

- **Ready for the first collisions.** In 2009–2010 I helped prepare the liquid-argon calorimeter for the first collisions at the LHC, writing software that monitors its electronics and a database for its trigger data.
- **Heavy neutrinos.** For my PhD (2013) I searched for a heavy "right-handed" W boson and heavy neutrinos, among the first searches of their kind at the LHC, and coordinated the heavy-neutrino analysis group.
- **Bottom quarks at high energy.** I proposed a new method to check how well ATLAS identifies jets coming from bottom quarks.
