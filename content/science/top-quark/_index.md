The top quark is the heaviest elementary particle we know of, about as heavy as a whole atom of gold, yet as far as we can tell it has no size at all. It lives for less than a trillionth of a trillionth of a second before turning into lighter particles, so we only ever see its traces in the detector.

Because it is so heavy, the top quark interacts most strongly with the Higgs boson, the particle that gives others their mass. That makes it one of the best places to look for signs of new physics that our current theory, the Standard Model, doesn't explain. I measure rare ways in which top quarks are produced and search for decays that the theory says should (almost) never happen: seeing one would be a discovery.
