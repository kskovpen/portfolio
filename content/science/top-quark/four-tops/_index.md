In proton collisions at the LHC, energy can turn into matter, sometimes into very heavy particles. Producing **four top quarks at once**, the heaviest combination ever seen at the collider, is extremely rare: it happens roughly once in every few trillion collisions. Finding these few events among billions of ordinary ones took years of data and careful analysis.

In 2023 the CMS experiment observed this process for the first time, and I was one of the main analysts behind the result. Why it matters: the rate at which four top quarks appear is very sensitive to how the top quark talks to the Higgs boson and to possible new particles too heavy to be produced directly. So far it agrees with our theory, the Standard Model, and every new measurement tightens the room left for surprises.
