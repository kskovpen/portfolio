SND is a detector at VEPP-2000, an electron–positron collider at the Budker Institute of Nuclear Physics in Novosibirsk. When an electron meets its antiparticle, the positron, both disappear and their energy turns into new particles. At VEPP-2000's energies these are light particles such as pions and kaons and, just above the threshold, a proton or a neutron together with its antiparticle.

The heart of SND is a sphere of more than 1,600 **sodium iodide crystals** in three layers that light up when particles hit them. It measures the energy of photons coming from the collision in almost every direction. A **drift chamber** inside follows charged particles, and an **iron absorber** and **muon system** outside identify the particles that get through.

## What I did here

This is where my research started, from my bachelor's thesis onwards. I measured how often electron–positron collisions produce a **neutron and an antineutron**, which tells us how the electric charge and magnetism are distributed inside the neutron. Antineutrons are hard to spot because they carry no charge, so I built a test setup of scintillator counters to measure how precisely the crystals time a particle's signal, and simulated it in detail.
