SHiP is a new experiment being prepared at CERN. It will look for *hidden* particles: particles so light and so weakly interacting that they could have slipped past every experiment so far, including those at the LHC. Such particles could explain dark matter, or why the universe contains more matter than antimatter.

Instead of colliding two beams, SHiP fires an intense beam of protons into a heavy metal **target**. Almost everything produced there is stopped right away or pushed aside by a **shield of magnets**. What remains flies into a long, empty **decay volume**. If a hidden particle decays inside it, the **spectrometer** and the **calorimeter** at the far end see its decay products appear out of nowhere. In front of the decay volume, a **neutrino detector** studies the neutrinos that the beam produces in huge numbers.

## What I do here

- I lead the Ghent University team in SHiP and am a member of the experiment's collaboration and technical boards.
- I lead the group building the calorimeter of the neutrino detector (the "Scattering and Neutrino Detector"), and the group building the upstream background tagger, which flags particles that enter the decay volume from outside so they are not mistaken for a discovery.
- With students and young researchers in Ghent, we design and test prototype detectors for both.
