Everything around us is made of a handful of tiny particles. To study them, physicists smash protons together at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) at CERN near Geneva. In each collision, energy turns into new, often very heavy particles that live for a tiny fraction of a second, and huge detectors photograph what they leave behind.

I work on two things: **analysing those collisions**, mostly ones that produce the top quark, the heaviest particle we know, and **building the detectors** that make the measurements possible, for the CMS experiment at CERN and for the new SHiP experiment.
