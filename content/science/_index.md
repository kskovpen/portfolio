Everything around us, from stars to our own bodies, is made of a handful of tiny particles held together by a few forces. Particle physics asks what those particles are and how they behave. It is fundamental research: it does not aim at a product, yet along the way it has given us the World Wide Web (invented at CERN), medical scanners and cancer therapy with particle beams.

To study the smallest particles, physicists smash them together at very high energy. In each collision, energy turns into new particles, often heavy ones that exist for only a tiny fraction of a second. Huge detectors, built like cameras around the collision point, record what they leave behind.

I have worked on four such experiments: **CMS** and **SHiP** at CERN today, and before that **ATLAS** at CERN and **SND** in Novosibirsk. Each page below shows how the experiment works, with a simplified drawing, and what I do there.
